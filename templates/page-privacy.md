The honest version first: I do not want your data. I want to know which pages
get read, so I write more of those. Nothing here is sold, profiled,
retargeted or whispered to an ad network - the numbers are used for
developing this site and for research into what is worth building next.

The version below is the same thing, said the way the law asks it to be said.
It applies the GDPR because you have rights here whether or not I find
paperwork fun.

## Who is responsible

Me - {owner}, the person behind {site}. The contact page lists the channels
that actually reach me; any of them works for anything on this page.

## What is measured, and only if you said yes

Nothing is counted until you press **Allow** on the consent bar. Declining is
not a negotiation - it is silence, permanently, until you change your mind.

If you did say yes, the site records page views: which page, when, what it
was referred from, and rough facts about the browser and device. The legal
basis is your consent (art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), and you can withdraw it at any
time - the cookies page says how.

## What is collected without asking

The server that delivers these pages keeps ordinary access logs for a short
time: IP address, requested URL, timestamp. The legal basis is legitimate
interest (art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) in keeping the site running, and the logs are
not used for anything else.

## How long

Analytics events are kept for at most twelve months and then deleted. Server
logs rotate away much sooner. There is no archive of you here.

## Your rights

All of them, the full GDPR set: access, rectification, erasure, restriction,
portability, and objection. Withdrawing consent never affects what happened
before you withdrew it - it just stops the counting from then on.

If you believe your data has been handled badly, you can complain to your
supervisory authority. I would appreciate hearing about it first.

## What this site will never do

> [!NOTE] The short list
> No ads. No selling data. No cross-site tracking. No fingerprinting. No
> newsletters you did not ask for. No dark patterns on the consent bar - the
> no button is exactly as big as the yes button, on purpose.
