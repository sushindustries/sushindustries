The honest version first: I do not want your data. I want to know which pages
get read, so I write more of those. That is the entire scheme. Nothing here is
sold, profiled, retargeted or whispered to an ad network - the numbers are
used for developing this site and for my own research into what is worth
building next.

The version below is the same thing, said the way the law asks it to be said.
It applies the GDPR - in Poland, RODO - because that is where I live and
because you have rights here whether or not I find paperwork fun.

## Who is responsible

Me - Adam Jurek, the one person behind this site. The [contact
page](/p/contact) lists the channels that actually reach me; any of them works
for anything on this page.

## What is measured, and only if you said yes

Nothing is counted until you press **Allow** on the consent bar. Declining is
not a negotiation - it is silence, permanently, until you change your mind.

If you did say yes, the site records page views - which page, when, what it
was referred from, and rough facts about the browser and device - and reports
of JavaScript errors the site throws in your browser, so I can fix them. The
legal basis is your consent (art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), and you can withdraw it at
any time - the [cookies page](/p/cookies) says how, and withdrawing is as
easy as agreeing was.

The counting is done by **PostHog**, running on their EU cloud, acting as a
processor. Events go to their servers in the European Union and stay there.

## What is collected without asking

The server that delivers these pages - hosted on **Railway** - keeps ordinary
access logs for a short time: IP address, requested URL, timestamp. Every web
server on earth does this; it is how problems get found and abuse gets
stopped. The legal basis is legitimate interest (art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) in
keeping the site running, and the logs are not used for anything else.

## How long

Analytics events are kept for at most twelve months and then deleted. Server
logs rotate away much sooner. There is no archive of you here.

## Your rights

All of them, the full GDPR set: access, rectification, erasure, restriction,
portability, and objection. Withdrawing consent never affects what happened
before you withdrew it - it just stops the counting from then on.

If you believe I have handled your data badly, you can complain to your
supervisory authority - in Poland that is the President of the Personal Data
Protection Office (UODO, [uodo.gov.pl](https://uodo.gov.pl)). I would
appreciate hearing about it first, because whatever it is, it is almost
certainly a mistake rather than a scheme.

## What this site will never do

> [!NOTE] The short list
> No ads. No selling data. No cross-site tracking. No fingerprinting. No
> newsletters you did not ask for. No dark patterns on the consent bar - the
> no button is exactly as big as the yes button, on purpose.
